### Cover letter (optional but recommended)

Dear medRxiv editors,

I am submitting this manuscript, "Biomedical signal forensics: a reliability framework for wearable-derived digital biomarkers, with real-data validation on WESAD," for pre-print posting.

The paper presents an open-source Python toolkit for auditing wearable physiological signals before downstream modeling, validates it on a 300-participant synthetic cohort and on the public WESAD dataset (n=15 subjects, 6,585 windows), and reports a head-to-head comparison against three published PPG signal-quality baselines (Orphanidou 2015, Sukor 2011, Elgendi 2016). The audit framework combines window-level artifact detection, participant-level reliability primitives, doubly-robust causal-adjusted confounding analysis, change-point surveillance, and stratified fairness disparity testing in a single reproducible pipeline.

The associated software (biomedical-signal-forensics-lab v0.16.2, MIT licensed, 235 passing tests across Python 3.11 and 3.12) has been simultaneously submitted to the Journal of Open Source Software. This medRxiv pre-print extends the JOSS software paper with a full methodology manuscript including TRIPOD+AI, STARD 2015, and FUTURE-AI compliance checklists, and the complete WESAD validation analysis at n=15 subjects.

The work declares no external funding and no competing interests. Code is reproducible end-to-end from a fixed seed in under three minutes on a laptop CPU.

## Possible screening rejections to anticipate

medRxiv's screening team checks for:

- Adequate methodological detail (yours has methods section with subsections 2.1-2.9)
- Ethics statement (present)
- Clear data and code availability (present, with three independent locations)
- Reasonable scope (a methodology paper with real-data validation is in scope)
- No personal patient data (n/a, secondary analysis)
- Not a duplicate submission (the JOSS software paper is a different paper about the software; disclose in cover letter)

Common gotchas that get bounced:

- Missing affiliation for the author (yours is present)
- Missing competing-interests declaration (yours is present)
- Cover letter that overstates clinical claims (yours is methodological, no clinical claims)
- Suggesting that the paper is a clinical trial (it is not, and yours correctly says so)

Low probability of screening rejection. If it happens, the rejection email will say why and you can revise and resubmit.
